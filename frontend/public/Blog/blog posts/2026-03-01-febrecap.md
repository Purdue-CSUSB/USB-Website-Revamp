With a brief taste of spring in the air, USB kicked off February with a series of engaging events for the CS community, offering a balance between wellness and professional growth.


On a typical day, the Lawson Commons is filled with TA office hours, the hum of collaboration, and the sound of markers gliding across whiteboards. But on Thursday, March 12th, the space looked a little different. Instead of laptops and homework, students carried flowers and handwritten cards as USB hosted its Love in Lawson event.

Organized by the [Social Wellness](https://www.purdueusb.com/initiatives/social-wellness) initiative, Love in Lawson was “a Valentine’s Day event for students to make their own bouquets, craft cards, and enjoy pizza,” said Anika Thapar, lead of the initiative.

“The intention behind this event was to create a space where students could enjoy spending time with friends, meet new people, and create something meaningful for a loved one,” Thapar shared.

With over 80 attendees, the energy in the room made the event’s success clear. When asked whether Love in Lawson would return, Thapar emphasized: “I really want Love in Lawson to become a yearly event. My goal with the Social Wellness Initiative is to host events like this that get students excited. I want people to have something to look forward to at the end of their days.”


While the month began with creativity and connection, USB also leaned into professional development through the [Career Resources Initiative](https://www.purdueusb.com/initiatives/career-resources), led by Netra Joshi.

The first event featured Lockheed Martin. “The USB x Lockheed Martin Information Session was an opportunity for students to learn about internship and full-time roles, and receive resume tips directly from a Lockheed Martin recruiter,” Joshi explained.

Students walked away with valuable insights. “Attendees learned more about Lockheed’s opportunities and what recruiters value most on a student’s resume,” she said.


A week later, USB partnered with Klaviyo for a similar professional development event. “The USB x Klaviyo Information Session and Resume Review gave Purdue students the chance to hear directly from Software Engineers at Klaviyo who are also Purdue alumni,” Joshi shared.

“The most memorable moment was seeing the level of engagement during the session. Every attendee had multiple thoughtful questions about Klaviyo.”


From creative expression in Lawson to meaningful career conversations with industry leaders, February was a dynamic month for USB. We’re excited to carry this momentum into March and continue building experiences that support both the well-being and professional growth of our CS community.
